About me

I love building products. Not the kind that just look good in Figma, the kind that solve real problems, feel right to use, and last beyond launch day. I'm drawn to the full cycle: understanding people, making decisions, designing systems, and caring enough about how things get built to stay close to the process until the end.

I approach each project with questions

Every project rewrites the process

I care about process. Every project I work on has one, and I lean on it to keep things clear for me and for the team. What changes is the shape of it. I adapt the process to the context, the constraints and what the team actually needs, instead of running the same playbook every time. No two projects look the same, some needed weeks of research before a single screen existed, others needed a working prototype by Friday.

Curiosity is my engine. I'm always poking at something new, a tool, a framework, a rabbit hole, and I tend to figure out fast which of those things is actually useful right now. AI has been part of my daily work for the last two years, but as an assistant, not as the main tool. Pen and paper are still where the thinking happens. I ask a lot of questions, usually before opening Figma. The hardest part of product design isn't the interface, it's making sure you're solving the right problem. I listen for what people don't say, and I'm comfortable working in ambiguity. While the answer isn't there yet, I go looking for it: talking to people, digging through data, testing a rough prototype, borrowing from whatever gets me closer. I don't wait for clarity, I chase it.

Product design is a wide umbrella: interaction, visual craft, systems thinking. But most of the job is communication, translating between business, engineering, and the people who'll actually use the thing. What I'm looking for is a team that builds close together, where design sits in the conversation from the start and not at the end of it.
